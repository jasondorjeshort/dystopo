# Tracks, phone-to-phone sync and sync on the phone

Design and numbers: `../PLANNING.md` › Sync. Schema changes: `.claude/rules/trackdb.md`.

## Tracks

**Tracks** (T31, T40; `shared/.../store/`, `androidApp/.../tracking/`, routes in `TrackRoutes.kt`, which documents every one). Recording goes through `TrackRecorder` into the SQLDelight `TrackStore` (`tracks.db`), one fix per insert. Pause holds fixes, resume starts a new segment, and a gap of more than `gapS` seconds between fixes (default 300, `POST /tracks/settings?gapS=`, 0 = never) starts one too when the walker also moved more than 50 m across it (T65: a long stop with no fixes, such as the Skyline fixture's 15 minutes at Panorama Point, keeps one segment). Stats are cached in the store and invalidated on append (`GET /tracks` reports `statsComputed`, the misses). GPX export writes our ids in a `dystopo:id` extension (namespace `GpxExtensions.NS_DYSTOPO`), and import (`POST /tracks/import`, GPX body or `?file=` from `files/tracks/`, or Import in the tracks list) keeps them and skips ids already stored. Test with a replay: `curl -X POST --data-binary @shared/src/commonTest/resources/gpx/skyline-loop.gpx "localhost:8686/location/replay?rate=100&record=Skyline"`, then `GET /tracks`, `POST /tracks/<id>/profile` (profile panel), `POST /tracks/waypoints/<wid>/select` (rename/delete sheet) and a `mode=window` screenshot. Replays keep the file's intervals; when the recording already holds a newer fix (recording started a moment before the replay), the replay is shifted onto the recording's timeline (`shiftedMs` in the response) so no point is dropped.

**Waypoints at any time** (T91; `tracking/Tracking.kt` `dropWaypoint`, `tracking/LongPressMenu.kt`, routes in `TrackRoutes.kt`). The map's Waypoint chip shows whether the Tracks bar is folded or not and whether a recording runs or not. It drops a waypoint at "here", the newer of the recorder's last fix and the map's last device fix: on the recording track while one records, else free-standing. The waypoint sheet then opens to name it. With no position yet, a note says to long-press the map instead. A long-press on the map opens a card with the pressed coordinates and three entries: "Waypoint here" (a waypoint at that point, elevation from the DEM pack, on the recording track or free-standing as above, then the sheet), "Route from here" (the T45 route start, so the next long-press is its end) and Cancel. While a route waits for its end or is planning, a long-press goes straight to the route, so planning is long-press, Route from here, long-press. The card takes the map's long-press through `RouteSession.overlay.onLongPress`. Free-standing waypoints are drawn whatever track is shown and sync like any other waypoint. Debug: `POST /waypoint[?lat=&lon=][&name=]` is the chip, or a given point. It answers the stored waypoint with `sheetOpen` and `drop`, 409 with no position, and 400 when only one of `lat` and `lon` is given. `POST /map/longpress?lat=&lon=` is a long-press. `POST /map/longpress/waypoint|route|cancel` taps the card (409 when it is not open), and `GET /map/longpress` gives its state (`open`, `lat`, `lon`, `presses`, `lastAction`, `routePhase`). `GET /tracks` has `freeWaypoints` and `lastDrop` (`id`, `track`, `via` `chip`, `longpress` or `route`, and `position` `given`, `recorderFix` or `deviceFix`). `POST /route/longpress` still feeds the route directly.

## Phone-to-phone sync

**Phone-to-phone sync** (T54; `androidApp/.../share/`, routes in `ShareRoutes.kt`). The Share screen ("Share" in the tracks list, or `POST /share/start`) opens a second listener bound to `0.0.0.0` (`share/ShareListener.kt` since T90: the client closes first and a reply that stops moving for 60 s is dropped; T96: the wait for the client's close is at most 2 min, all on one watcher thread; when 32 sockets wait, one more closes the oldest idle one, T106: one that has waited 5 s plus its body at 256 KiB/s, so a reply still draining through a relay is not cut; with none idle it waits too, up to 256, where the oldest goes anyway; counters as `listener` in `GET /share/status`, with `lingerEvicted`, `lingerEvictedHard` and `lingerPeak`) (port 8841, else any free port) that answers only `POST /share/sealed` (T72): `/share/hello` and the T44 `/sync/pull|push|status` routes travel inside it, ChaCha20-Poly1305-encrypted under the 16-character share code on screen (`7K3M-QW2D-9XHP-T4CA`; a wrong code is a 403 and five within ten minutes replace the code (T106: older wrong codes are forgotten, `failuresAged` in the gate's counters; T110: counted per address, so five from one address within ten minutes lock that address out, 429 "too many wrong codes from this phone" without opening its requests, until they age or the code changes, while other phones go on; fifty from all addresses together still replace the code; `addressesFailing`, `addressesLockedOut`, `lockedRefused` in `gate`), a replayed request a 409, the old plain routes a 426), announces `_dystopo._tcp` over NSD, lists other phones, and draws a QR of `dystopo://share?addr=<host:port>&code=<code>`. Closing the screen (Back, Close, `POST /share/stop`) closes the port. `POST /share/connect?addr=<host[:port]|share link>&code=` checks the code, then runs one pull-merge-push; `GET /share/status` has the code, port, refusals, NSD state and everything discovered; `GET /share/qr` the QR matrix. Two emulators on one host share a virtual Wi-Fi network (`wlan0`, 10.0.2.18, 10.0.2.20, ...), so NSD discovery works and a direct connect to the peer's `10.0.2.x:8841` works when both have a Wi-Fi carrier (T90; an emulator that never gets one can only go through the host: restart it). Through the host: `adb -s <A> forward tcp:8841 tcp:<A's share port>`, then on B `POST /share/connect?addr=10.0.2.2:8841&code=<A's code>` (10.0.2.2 is the host from an emulator); remove with `adb -s <A> forward --remove tcp:8841`. T57: both sides use `AppSync.replica`, and a connect runs through `AppSync.run` (paged like any HTTP sync, `&page=`; conflicts, synced settings, the clock check). A tapped `dystopo://share?addr=&code=` link (`adb shell am start -a android.intent.action.VIEW -d "'dystopo://share?addr=10.0.2.2:8841&code=7K3MQW2D9XHPT4CA'"`) or the in-app scanner ("Scan QR code" on the Share screen: CameraX frames, our own pure-Kotlin decoder `share/QrDecoder.kt`, no ML Kit) fills the address and code; the user still taps Sync. `POST /share/scan` with a PNG or JPEG body (a screenshot of the other phone's Share screen) runs the same decoder and fills the fields; `GET /share/scan` has the camera scanner's frames and reads. **Via server** (T72, `share/RelaySession.kt`, `ShareRelayRoutes.kt`; design and threat model in docs/PLANNING.md › Sync): phones on different networks sync through the server's encrypted relay (`/sync/relay`, docs/SERVER.md › The relay): `POST /share/relay/start[?server=][&code=]` (no code: a new one; the other phone passes it as `code=`) posts this phone's whole log sealed under the code, merges what the other phone posted, then polls every 5 s while the blob lives (15 min) and syncs again when something new arrives; `POST /share/relay/sync`, `POST /share/relay/stop[?forget=1]`, `GET /share/relay` (code, server, polls, syncs, blobs received and rejected, last result). On the screen: New code, or type the other phone's code and Join. Emulators need no adb forward for this: both reach the server at `http://10.0.2.2:<port>`. **Peer sources** (T78; `share/PeerServe.kt`, `PeerSources.kt`, `PeerFiles.kt`, routes in `share/PeerRoutes.kt`; design and numbers in docs/PLANNING.md › Sync › Peer sources on a trip): while the Share screen is open the listener also publishes, sealed under the code, the phone's pack catalog (server `/catalog` format with SHA-256, `/peer/catalog`), its area manifests (`/peer/areas[/<id>]`), archive pieces (`/peer/file/<file>?from=&len=`) and pack tiles (`/dem`, `/osm`); the Share screen shows "Maps sent from this phone" (`serving` in `/share/status`). The other phone adds it with "Use its maps" or `POST /share/peers/add?addr=&code=`, or by discovery with a remembered code (`POST /share/peers/code?code=`: tried once on every phone NSD resolves, within the T96 limits below; `catalogAfterDiscoveryMs` in `GET /share/peers`); the peer becomes a `peer://<host:port>` source of the online fetcher, asked before the network, and `POST /share/peers/download?peer=&pack=[&activate=1]` (or Download on the Share screen) fetches a pack through the normal downloader (resume, SHA-256; the job's `catalog.server` is the `peer://` URL), `POST /share/peers/area?peer=&id=` an area. Also `/share/peers/refresh|via|bench|sealbench|testframe`, `GET /share/peers/catalog`, `DELETE /share/peers?peer=`. Two emulators connect directly over their shared virtual Wi-Fi (10.0.2.x:8841) for this; to prove the bytes came from the peer, root the receiver (`adb root`, google_apis images) and reject its app uid's traffic to anything but loopback and the peer's address with `iptables -m owner --uid-owner <uid>` (T78's run). Debug builds on an emulator default to the coordinator's server (`http://10.0.2.2:8765`): set `POST /settings?server=` on a phone that must have no source. T90: peers are kept across restarts (`files/share/peers.json`, loaded when the map screen starts; `restoredPeers` in `GET /share/peers`, each peer's `state` `connected`, `saved`, `unreachable` or `codeChanged`, `lastSeenAt`, `retries`, `nextRetryAt`; the Share screen shows "last seen" and Forget); `POST /share/peers/piece?peer=&file=&from=&len=` fetches one piece of the peer's archive as a download does and reports its SHA-256 and speed. T96 (`share/PeerCodes.kt`), because five wrong codes replace the other phone's code: a code a phone refused (403) is never sent to that phone again by any path (retry, discovery, refresh, download, a tile), and the refusal is kept in `peers.json` across restarts; a kept peer's code goes only to that phone (recognised by address, NSD name or the device id in its NSD record), and it is the only code from before that phone gets; any other phone gets at most one code kept from before per app start; codes learned since the start (`POST /share/peers/code`, "Use its maps") are tried once on each phone. Every request to a phone goes through one gate per address and code: when the code has not answered for a second, one request goes first and the others (a map view's tiles, a download's pieces) wait for its answer, then go together, fail unsent if it was refused, or fail at once with its error if there was no connection, so a burst after the other phone reopened its screen sends one wrong code (T96's verifier saw seven from one cold map view). T106: within that second at most four requests with one code are out to a phone before one of them answers (`PeerCodes.MAX_UNANSWERED`; a 222 MB pack downloaded from the other emulator at 1.9 MB/s with it and without it), so a code a third phone made the other one replace meanwhile costs at most four failures; only a reply confirms a code (a request that died with an `Error` used to count as one). A peer added by hand stays one (`auto` false) when discovery reaches the same phone at another address. A peer whose own code is refused is `codeChanged` (also when it was connected): its retry is cancelled, it stops being a fetcher source, and refresh or download answer 403 `wrongCode` without sending anything; Forget (`DELETE /share/peers`) retires its code. `GET /share/peers` has `codes` (`known`, `fresh`, `refused`, `sent`, `held`, and `waited` and `spared`: requests that waited for another's answer, and those then not sent). The Share screen does not show a download that failed before the peer's state last changed. Two emulators with root can be kept from other agents' phones on the shared virtual Wi-Fi with `iptables` (`-A OUTPUT -d 10.0.2.0/24 -p tcp -j REJECT` after accepting the pair, the host 10.0.2.2 and DNS 10.0.2.3; on the sharer, `--dport 8841` accepted only from the pair and 127.0.0.1): discovery resolves every Dystopo phone with its Share screen open, and each wrong code tried spends one of that phone's failures. Testing a slow or relayed path: the emulator's network throttling (`-NetSpeed`, `emu network speed`) did not slow either path measurably on emulator 37 with API 35 (2.3 to 7.5 MB/s at `gsm`), but turning Wi-Fi off on the receiver (`adb shell svc wifi disable`; 10.0.2.2 stays reachable over `eth0`) sends it through the emulator's user-mode network, where the sharer's close cut replies before T90. `python -I tools/android-probe.py --port <debug forward> --serial <serial> --share` probes the share listener (plain routes 426, wrong code 403, replay 409, malformed requests).

## Sync

**Sync** (T44, T51; design and numbers in `docs/PLANNING.md` › Sync). User data is an operation log (`shared/.../sync/`) that the track store materialises; replicas exchange bundles. The always-on peer is `devtool serve` (and the `server` module) at `/sync/*`, replica in `out/sync/peer.db` (`DYSTOPO_SYNC_DB`). Check convergence with `./gradlew :devtool:run --args="sync-sim --peers 3 --ops 2000 --seed 42 --partitions 5 --reorder --seeds 20 --skew-days 2 --crashes 3"` (`--store sql` for SQLite peers, `--page 50` for paged pulls; exit 1 and a `replay` line on a failure). A JVM replica: `devtool sync status|with|export|import|import-gpx|rename|gpx|gen --db <file>` (`with --peer http://127.0.0.1:8687 --page 2000 [--token T]`; `gen --ops 50000` writes a big synthetic log for paging numbers). Phone (debug): `POST /sync?with=http://10.0.2.2:<port>` (pages of 2000; `&page=0` for one bundle), `GET /sync/status` (digest, `pendingOps`, `conflicts`, `clockSkew`, `settingsSync`), `POST /sync/settings?peer=&token=`, `GET|DELETE /sync/conflicts`, `POST /sync/ui?open=1` (the status sheet, also "Sync status" in the tracks list). Units, text scale and map options set on one device reach the others through the log (`app.*` settings). On API 35 emulators keep wifi enabled, or 10.0.2.2 is unreachable. T57: every sync compares clocks with the peer (the server's HTTP `Date` header, a phone's `now` in `/share/hello`); more than 2 minutes apart beyond the measurement's uncertainty is a warning in the result line and on the sync sheet (`clockCheck` in `/sync/status`). The sync sheet also edits the peer and the bearer token (never shown back). T65 (`sync/RouteOps.kt`): a route's waypoints are written one per field (`wpt:<waypoint id>`, null = removed), so concurrent waypoint edits on two devices merge (the same waypoint: last writer wins); T108: a trip's turn directions are a `directions` field written after each path write, counted only while the path they were made for is the route's path (PLANNING › Routing; builds before T108 ignore the field and forward it); compaction drops route field writes superseded by a newer write of the same field, waypoint writes excepted (the log keeps one `path` per route, `CompactResult.opsDropped`); a `path` value over 256 KiB is refused with `RouteTooLargeException` ("split it into shorter routes").
