# Tracks, phone-to-phone sync and sync on the phone

Design and numbers: `../PLANNING.md` › Sync. Schema changes: `.claude/rules/trackdb.md`.

## Tracks

**Tracks** (T31, T40; `shared/.../store/`, `androidApp/.../tracking/`, routes in `TrackRoutes.kt`, which documents every one). Recording goes through `TrackRecorder` into the SQLDelight `TrackStore` (`tracks.db`), one fix per insert. Pause holds fixes, resume starts a new segment, and a gap of more than `gapS` seconds between fixes (default 300, `POST /tracks/settings?gapS=`, 0 = never) starts one too when the walker also moved more than 50 m across it (T65: a long stop with no fixes, such as the Skyline fixture's 15 minutes at Panorama Point, keeps one segment). Stats are cached in the store and invalidated on append (`GET /tracks` reports `statsComputed`, the misses). GPX export writes our ids in a `dystopo:id` extension (namespace `GpxExtensions.NS_DYSTOPO`), and import (`POST /tracks/import`, GPX body or `?file=` from `files/tracks/`, or Import in the tracks list) keeps them and skips ids already stored. Test with a replay: `curl -X POST --data-binary @shared/src/commonTest/resources/gpx/skyline-loop.gpx "localhost:8686/location/replay?rate=100&record=Skyline"`, then `GET /tracks`, `POST /tracks/<id>/profile` (profile panel), `POST /tracks/waypoints/<wid>/select` (rename/delete sheet) and a `mode=window` screenshot. Replays keep the file's intervals; when the recording already holds a newer fix (recording started a moment before the replay), the replay is shifted onto the recording's timeline (`shiftedMs` in the response) so no point is dropped.

## Phone-to-phone sync

**Phone-to-phone sync** (T54; `androidApp/.../share/`, routes in `ShareRoutes.kt`). The Share screen ("Share" in the tracks list, or `POST /share/start`) opens a second `LocalServer` bound to `0.0.0.0` (port 8841, else any free port) that answers only `POST /share/sealed` (T72): `/share/hello` and the T44 `/sync/pull|push|status` routes travel inside it, ChaCha20-Poly1305-encrypted under the 16-character share code on screen (`7K3M-QW2D-9XHP-T4CA`; a wrong code is a 403 and five replace the code, a replayed request a 409, the old plain routes a 426), announces `_dystopo._tcp` over NSD, lists other phones, and draws a QR of `dystopo://share?addr=<host:port>&code=<code>`. Closing the screen (Back, Close, `POST /share/stop`) closes the port. `POST /share/connect?addr=<host[:port]|share link>&code=` checks the code, then runs one pull-merge-push; `GET /share/status` has the code, port, refusals, NSD state and everything discovered; `GET /share/qr` the QR matrix. Two emulators on one host share a virtual Wi-Fi network (`wlan0`, 10.0.2.18, 10.0.2.20, ...), so NSD discovery works, but a direct connect to the peer's `10.0.2.x:8841` is not guaranteed (it worked for the implementer and failed for the verifier on the same host). The tested path goes through the host: `adb -s <A> forward tcp:8841 tcp:<A's share port>`, then on B `POST /share/connect?addr=10.0.2.2:8841&code=<A's code>` (10.0.2.2 is the host from an emulator); remove with `adb -s <A> forward --remove tcp:8841`. T57: both sides use `AppSync.replica`, and a connect runs through `AppSync.run` (paged like any HTTP sync, `&page=`; conflicts, synced settings, the clock check). A tapped `dystopo://share?addr=&code=` link (`adb shell am start -a android.intent.action.VIEW -d "'dystopo://share?addr=10.0.2.2:8841&code=7K3MQW2D9XHPT4CA'"`) or the in-app scanner ("Scan QR code" on the Share screen: CameraX frames, our own pure-Kotlin decoder `share/QrDecoder.kt`, no ML Kit) fills the address and code; the user still taps Sync. `POST /share/scan` with a PNG or JPEG body (a screenshot of the other phone's Share screen) runs the same decoder and fills the fields; `GET /share/scan` has the camera scanner's frames and reads. **Via server** (T72, `share/RelaySession.kt`, `ShareRelayRoutes.kt`; design and threat model in docs/PLANNING.md › Sync): phones on different networks sync through the server's encrypted relay (`/sync/relay`, docs/SERVER.md › The relay): `POST /share/relay/start[?server=][&code=]` (no code: a new one; the other phone passes it as `code=`) posts this phone's whole log sealed under the code, merges what the other phone posted, then polls every 5 s while the blob lives (15 min) and syncs again when something new arrives; `POST /share/relay/sync`, `POST /share/relay/stop[?forget=1]`, `GET /share/relay` (code, server, polls, syncs, blobs received and rejected, last result). On the screen: New code, or type the other phone's code and Join. Emulators need no adb forward for this: both reach the server at `http://10.0.2.2:<port>`. **Peer sources** (T78; `share/PeerServe.kt`, `PeerSources.kt`, `PeerFiles.kt`, routes in `share/PeerRoutes.kt`; design and numbers in docs/PLANNING.md › Sync › Peer sources on a trip): while the Share screen is open the listener also publishes, sealed under the code, the phone's pack catalog (server `/catalog` format with SHA-256, `/peer/catalog`), its area manifests (`/peer/areas[/<id>]`), archive pieces (`/peer/file/<file>?from=&len=`) and pack tiles (`/dem`, `/osm`); the Share screen shows "Maps sent from this phone" (`serving` in `/share/status`). The other phone adds it with "Use its maps" or `POST /share/peers/add?addr=&code=`, or by discovery with a remembered code (`POST /share/peers/code?code=`: tried once on every phone NSD resolves; `catalogAfterDiscoveryMs` in `GET /share/peers`); the peer becomes a `peer://<host:port>` source of the online fetcher, asked before the network, and `POST /share/peers/download?peer=&pack=[&activate=1]` (or Download on the Share screen) fetches a pack through the normal downloader (resume, SHA-256; the job's `catalog.server` is the `peer://` URL), `POST /share/peers/area?peer=&id=` an area. Also `/share/peers/refresh|via|bench|sealbench|testframe`, `GET /share/peers/catalog`, `DELETE /share/peers?peer=`. Two emulators connect directly over their shared virtual Wi-Fi (10.0.2.x:8841) for this; to prove the bytes came from the peer, root the receiver (`adb root`, google_apis images) and reject its app uid's traffic to anything but loopback and the peer's address with `iptables -m owner --uid-owner <uid>` (T78's run). Debug builds on an emulator default to the coordinator's server (`http://10.0.2.2:8765`): set `POST /settings?server=` on a phone that must have no source. `python -I tools/android-probe.py --port <debug forward> --serial <serial> --share` probes the share listener (plain routes 426, wrong code 403, replay 409, malformed requests).

## Sync

**Sync** (T44, T51; design and numbers in `docs/PLANNING.md` › Sync). User data is an operation log (`shared/.../sync/`) that the track store materialises; replicas exchange bundles. The always-on peer is `devtool serve` (and the `server` module) at `/sync/*`, replica in `out/sync/peer.db` (`DYSTOPO_SYNC_DB`). Check convergence with `./gradlew :devtool:run --args="sync-sim --peers 3 --ops 2000 --seed 42 --partitions 5 --reorder --seeds 20 --skew-days 2 --crashes 3"` (`--store sql` for SQLite peers, `--page 50` for paged pulls; exit 1 and a `replay` line on a failure). A JVM replica: `devtool sync status|with|export|import|import-gpx|rename|gpx|gen --db <file>` (`with --peer http://127.0.0.1:8687 --page 2000 [--token T]`; `gen --ops 50000` writes a big synthetic log for paging numbers). Phone (debug): `POST /sync?with=http://10.0.2.2:<port>` (pages of 2000; `&page=0` for one bundle), `GET /sync/status` (digest, `pendingOps`, `conflicts`, `clockSkew`, `settingsSync`), `POST /sync/settings?peer=&token=`, `GET|DELETE /sync/conflicts`, `POST /sync/ui?open=1` (the status sheet, also "Sync status" in the tracks list). Units, text scale and map options set on one device reach the others through the log (`app.*` settings). On API 35 emulators keep wifi enabled, or 10.0.2.2 is unreachable. T57: every sync compares clocks with the peer (the server's HTTP `Date` header, a phone's `now` in `/share/hello`); more than 2 minutes apart beyond the measurement's uncertainty is a warning in the result line and on the sync sheet (`clockCheck` in `/sync/status`). The sync sheet also edits the peer and the bearer token (never shown back). T65 (`sync/RouteOps.kt`): a route's waypoints are written one per field (`wpt:<waypoint id>`, null = removed), so concurrent waypoint edits on two devices merge (the same waypoint: last writer wins); compaction drops route field writes superseded by a newer write of the same field, waypoint writes excepted (the log keeps one `path` per route, `CompactResult.opsDropped`); a `path` value over 256 KiB is refused with `RouteTooLargeException` ("split it into shorter routes").
