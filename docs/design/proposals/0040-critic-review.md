# Critic review of proposal 0040: stops and reroute

Date: 2026-10-23. Reviewed as it stands on `task/T132` (commit 8d842469). Read in this order: `CLAUDE.md`,
the proposal, PLANNING › Routing, ADR 0023, 0025, 0028, 0031, 0033, 0039, the T114 finding, the idea
files, and the code the proposal cites in `shared` and `androidApp`. Web sources re-read where they
answered (Google's help, Locus's blog and manual, the Apple stops blog); Apple's guide body and
AllTrails's page still did not load, so those two rows stay as snippets.

## Verdict in one line

Option A is the right choice and B and C are rightly rejected; the format half of the proposal is
sound and verified against the code. The weaknesses are in what the proposal leaves unsaid about the
plan's geometry during a trip (objections 1, 2, 3) and in refusals without a lift (4). None blocks.

## Objections

1. **Serious. Where a mid-trip replan's geometry lives is not stated.** The Reroute row says "the new first leg replaces the old" and the Where-plans-run row that "the navigation session takes a replaced path"; nothing says whether the kept trip's synced `path` (and with it `stops` and `directions`, three operations, a path value up to 256 KiB) is rewritten on every replan, or only the navigation session and `route-session.json`. If it is rewritten: a detour permanently changes the saved trip on every replica, and a bad-GPS day writes a path every 30 s into the log and every bundle until compaction. If it is not: "the plan's geometry is recomputed from wherever the walker is" never persists, and the proposal must say the kept trip keeps its planned line. Evidence: the `directions` precedent only ever follows a user edit (`Trips.edit`); nothing in the code writes a path from navigation today. Resolves: one sentence in the Recommendation (my suggestion: a replan writes nothing to the store; a kept plan's `path` changes only by an edit of its stops or modes) and a `nav replay` assertion that the store's op count is unchanged after a replan.

2. **Serious. The invariant "every writer of `path` on a plan writes `stops`" is left to callers.** The key makes a forgotten write harmless to sync, as Option A says, but not to the user: the plan silently becomes a frozen route and its stop names and leg modes vanish. Today's path writers are `Trips.edit`, `Trips.create`, `Trips.keep`, `Trips.import`, the browser's Trips box, the server's planner, `putRoutePlan` in two stores and `migrateDirections`; the proposal adds Go's first-leg replan and the stale-fix replan, which change the first via point and therefore the key. The `directions` precedent already shows the failure mode: the browser does not write them and a trip edited there loses them (`Trips.kt:365`). Resolves: make the invariant structural, not procedural: `SyncStore.setRoutePath(id, path, stops, directions, now)` takes the plan's side fields in one transaction (an older build's call site cannot exist), and a store-contract test that writes a path to a plan without `stops` fails. Say in the proposal which writers exist and that the browser degrades plans until it writes the field.

3. **Serious. The "over 5 s" trigger is already met, so the replan must be asynchronous from the start.** "What would change" says an asynchronous replan with a Replanning state is needed only if a cold drive replan measures over 5 s. PLANNING › Routing, T77, measured 13.2 s on the phone for Paradise to Yakima by drive, and T76's 2 s is for a hike with the graph file already built (2.6 s to build it). A replan runs inside a navigation session fed by every fix; a synchronous 2 s call there is already wrong. Resolves: move the asynchronous replan and the Replanning state into the Recommendation, state that the 30 s rate limit counts from the replan's end, and drop the bullet or restate it as a prediction that can fail ("a hike replan inside one pack with the graph built measures under 2 s on the API 35 emulator; the drive case is the asynchronous one").

4. **Serious. Two refusals have no lift and no message that says so.** (a) The 30 s rate limit: `POST /nav/replan` lifts it for an agent; the walker has nothing, and the banner text while a replan is held back is not specified. Resolves: a "Replan now" button on the off-route banner that ignores the limit (a human tap is the lift), and the banner line saying when the next automatic one may run. (b) The cap of 25: no message, no lift, nowhere. Resolves: the message names the number ("A plan holds at most 25 stops"); the cap is a constant the owner changes, say so. The other mechanisms pass: the offline fallback's lift is the crow-leg button on the banner, in the moment; the Go dialogue's lift is "Follow anyway", once per start; the setting is in Settings › Navigation, reachable while navigating (the navigation mode closes the editor sheet, not Settings, `NavSession.kt:177`). One more line is needed for the offline fallback: a replan can also fail with graph data present when no trail lies within `snapM` of the fix (the walker is off the network, bushwhacking's case); the notice should say "no trail near you" rather than "no map data here", as the router's local failure already names the nearest trail (T77).

5. **Minor. A second reach rule beside the one that exists.** The Passed row proposes 30 m radius or 100 m beyond the projection; `Navigator` already advances `passed` for waypoints projected along the line at 15 m before their projection, only while on route (`Navigator.kt:180`). A stop is a via point on the line, so the existing along-line rule covers "walked past it", and the 30 m radius adds the corner-cutter. Two rules mean a waypoint placed at a stop and the stop itself can disagree on the trip card. Resolves: say which single rule both use (the coordinator's number), or why stops differ.

6. **Minor. `path.mode` for a plan is unnamed.** With per-leg modes in `stops`, the path still carries one `mode`, which older builds use for the summary (`RouteSummary.of`), snapping and editing, and which `RouteEditor.directions` uses to decide whether a splice is allowed (`before.mode == path.mode`). Resolves: state the rule (first leg's mode, or the mode of most of the distance) and that per-leg directions are regenerated whole, not spliced.

7. **Minor. Hidden coupling to the plan's offline download and to 0041.** `Trips.planOffline` builds the corridor of the current path (ADR 0027). A plan replanned from wherever the walker is leaves that corridor exactly when the fallback fires. Resolves: a line saying the plan's offline download is the current path's corridor plus a box around each stop (a polygon area when 0041 lands), or that replans beyond it fall to the fallback by design.

8. **Minor. The stop between two modes.** A trailhead stop ends a drive leg and starts a hike leg; `snap(lat, lon, mode)` snaps to one network and the router pins the leg ends to the via point exactly (`RouteEditor.kt:101`). Resolves: name the rule (snap a stop to the network of the leg that leaves it, so the trailhead sits on the trail and the drive leg's last metres are a straight connector) and show it in the PR's screenshot.

9. **Minor. "Schema 6" should read "the next free step".** Nine implementers are in flight; whichever task lands a `.sqm` first takes 5. The rule in `.claude/rules/trackdb.md` fixes the number at build time.

10. **Minor. Prior art corrections.** Apple's mid-trip "next stop" rule, the one load-bearing fact for the insertion default, is unverified (guide body unreadable twice; the blog does not say); mark it so and keep the default as the owner's position it already is. Add Locus's "Point priority" (recalculate to the nearest via point) as the precedent for replanning to the next stop, and note its automatic recalculation is a paid feature. Apple's cap: the guide snippet says 14, the blog 15; write "14 or 15".

11. **Minor. Bushwhacking's version step: two things to state.** (a) The claim that a new `LegKind` forces a version is true in effect but for a different reason than given: `LegKind.of` throws on an unknown code, so the whole path fails to decode and the older build shows the put's points as one fixed leg, the same outcome as an unknown version; the proposal should say that and name the reserved code (none is named). (b) Per-leg modes in a side field lose ADR 0033 Decision 3's protection for a leg: an older build never sees a leg's `bushwhack` mode, edits the plan freely, and reroutes that leg along trails; the result is the degrade rule's frozen route, not a misread, so it is acceptable, but the bushwhack proposal's safety wording cannot ride on the T108 refusal for a leg inside a plan. Say so, so the next proposal does not assume it.

12. **Minor. The sync-sim fixture line is ambiguous.** "A fixture written by v0.1.10-alpha that round-trips byte for byte and gains the field" cannot be one test: a v0.1.10 bundle holds no `stops`. Two tests, as T108 did: the old fixture materialises as a frozen route and re-encodes byte for byte; a `stops` write merged into a replica whose field set lacks it is ignored, kept and forwarded (`RouteDirectionsTest` lines 158 to 181, with `stops` in place of `directions`).

## The case against A, and the best alternative

The strongest case against A is objection 2 generalised: a plan is the intent and its geometry is derived, yet A stores the intent in a field keyed to the geometry, so every automatic geometry change threatens the intent, and every writer must know. B stores one value and has no such coupling, and its only real defect is the older-build edit winning by last writer. That defect could be closed inside B with a refusal rule (a `path` write of version 1 or 2 over a live version 3 is ignored on every replica), but a v0.1.10 build cannot carry the rule, so for one release the plan still dies on the older phone's edit; after that release B is the cleaner model. C has no coupling and no format change but fails the owner's first example. A is the right choice now because the one release behind is the one that matters in alpha; the proposal should record that at the first beta (ADR 0033 §5, when format changes are rolled out to testers together) folding `stops` into a path version 3 is the natural end state, with A's exit cost of "drop the field" then becoming "fold the field". Nothing in A decides bushwhacking's leg geometry (objection 11 names what it does decide), 0041's areas (objection 7 is a coupling, not a decision) or the detail-level setting (directions per leg filter on priority as today).

## Pre-mortem

Ten years on, plans are the feature users say "sometimes loses my stops". The keyed side field made the plan a second-class record: the invariant that every `path` write carries its `stops` lived in the heads of the agents who built the first task, and the browser's Trips box, the GPX import, a later server planner and two later features each wrote a path without it once; every time the plan silently became a frozen route, which looked like a bug in sync rather than a missing write, and the format epoch that would have folded the stops into the path (ADR 0033 §5, the first beta) came and went because nothing hurt enough on any one day. The line that should have caught it is Option A's own risk sentence, "two values that must agree (the key makes disagreement harmless, not impossible to write)": harmless to the log, not to the user. Objection 2's structural store call, and a dated line in the ADR that names the beta as the moment to fold the field, are what would have changed the story.

## Recommendation

Accept A with the Recommendation amended for objections 1 to 4 (where the replan's geometry lives; `setRoutePath` taking the plan's side fields; asynchronous replan from the start, the 5 s bullet rewritten as a prediction that can fail; "Replan now" on the banner and a cap message) and the minor lines folded in; promote to ADR 0040 after the revision, with the beta-time fold of `stops` into the path recorded under consequences. Confidence: high on the format choice (every mechanism it leans on is in the code and tested), medium on the policies (the numbers are inherited from T61 and the survey, which is right in alpha; the passed rule and the stop-snap rule are the two most likely to change once a device tour runs). Residual risks after the revision: the per-leg list at font scale 2.0 with many stops; the browser degrading plans until it writes the field; the Apple insertion rule resting on a snippet.
