## Part 2. The case against the recommendation, and the best alternative

The proposal's load-bearing promise is "the heap is bounded by a cell count known before allocation and admitted by `HeapGuard`, so the finding's failure cannot recur". That sentence holds only if the bytes per cell are right, and the proposal describes two different searches without choosing. Pass 2 "runs A* over a state space with two kinds of node: the graph's nodes (every graph tile the search reaches, as today, through the 32 MB LRU) and the cells", and the heuristic argument leans on `TileRouter`'s potentials. `TileRouter.Search` keeps, per node, `dist` in both directions (16 B), `parent` (8), `via` (8), `closed` (2) and `pot` (8): 42 B (`SEARCH_BYTES_PER_NODE`, `Router.kt` line 1125), plus the two heaps at 12 B an entry with lazy deletion (several entries per node). Hosting a million cells there is about 50 B a cell, 70 MB at the cap, not 24. The memory paragraph instead describes a fresh search (one `g` as `Float`, one parent, two flag bits, a heap "of cell indexes (4 B each)", which cannot order itself without a key array or a recomputed `f`): 16 B a cell, but then the graph side of pass 2 (tile loading, boundary twins, levels, the stop rule) is written a second time beside `TileRouter`, unidirectional, with a heuristic that is weak against costs inflated by 1.3 to 3.0 factors and so settles most of the window. Either design can work; the proposal must pick one, fit the weight the way T149 did (measured peak over cells, times a margin), and set the cap in bytes from that. My preference is the first: one search, one optimality argument, one code path for twins and levels, the grid as one more `GraphPiece`-like block of ids with its own `potential`, portals as `expandEnd`-style relaxations to both ends of the edge (`cost(edge, f, 0) + P` and `cost(edge, f, 1) + P`) and back from a node along each adjacent edge to the portals on it, `Step(piece, e, f0, f1)` already carrying partial edges for reconstruction. The price is 50 B a cell, so the z13 cap is about 500 k cells for 24 MB (or the bytes rise to 35 to 40 MB for 750 k and the admission says so); the 10 km row still fits at z13, the 20 and 30 km rows coarsen to z12 at once. That is a smaller promise than the table makes, and an honest one.

The second structural weakness is the impassable mask against the 16-neighbour moves. A knight's move from `a` to `b` crosses two intermediate cells, a diagonal passes between two diagonally adjacent cells; the proposal checks class and slope at `b` only ("classFactor(b)", "the Horn slope at b"). A cliff line from `natural_line` rasterised at z13 is one cell wide; a stream from `water` likewise; a knight's move steps over both, a diagonal slips between the corners of a diagonal line. `r.walk` has exactly this known defect. The fix is cheap (an impassable mask dilated to 8-connectivity for lines, and a knight's move allowed only when the cells it crosses are passable) and must be in the model before any PNG is believed.

The two-pass window does what the idea asks within its bound, but the bound is sized from the wrong length. `W = clamp(0.3 x D, 500 m, 3 km)` takes `D` as the straight distance. The idea's canonical case is a trail horseshoe: `D` = 2 km, the trail 12 km, the saddle 800 m off the straight line and 700 m off the trail. `W` = 600 m, the cut is in neither buffer, and the route stays 12 km with no notice that anything was missed. The saving at stake is the pass 1 length minus `D`; `W` should grow with it (for example `clamp(0.3 x max(D, pass1 / 2), 500 m, 3 km)`), and when pass 1 fails off-network the proposal could snap pass 1 at `FAR_SEARCH_M` (10 km, `Router.kt` line 377) to still get a trail corridor rather than fall to the straight line alone. With that, the window finds the cuts a walker would look for: near the line or near the trail.

The best alternative to the recommendation as a whole is not A, B, D or E; it is C with the four corrections above and the format and source corrections below (objections 5, 6, 7). A (end caps) is C's first milestone and should be the first task's first PNG, but it is not a different decision. The survey's setting-inside-hike has one real advantage the proposal undersells (one control instead of a chip plus a setting) and one real disadvantage the proposal states correctly (a hike-mode route with off-trail stretches is edited on an older build and re-routed along trails without a word). On balance the mode is right, for routes; inside plans neither design protects anything (ADR 0040), and the ADR should say the safety wording sits on the planning build's card alone.

## Part 3. Objections

Blocking: none. Serious: 10. Minor: 12.

1. **Serious. The search is two designs at once and the memory bound fits neither.** Part 1 C's text and heuristic lean on `TileRouter`'s bidirectional `Search` (42 B a node plus heaps, `Router.kt` lines 701 to 986, 1125); the memory paragraph assumes a fresh unidirectional search at 16 B a cell with an unkeyed heap. `HeapGuard` admits what it is asked for (claim 1), so an estimate low by three times recreates the finding's failure on the 512 MB phone with the guard saying `admitted`. *Resolve:* choose (my recommendation: host the grid in `TileRouter.Search` as an id block with its own potential, portals as partial-edge relaxations to both ends plus `P`); measure the peak per cell on the emulator in the first task with `HeapGuard.watch` as T149 did and fit the weight with a margin; set the cell cap from the measured bytes, not the other way round; state in the ADR that `graphBytes` of the tiles the search touches (today's `memory` field) counts in the same admission.

2. **Serious. Knight's moves and diagonals hop one-cell barriers.** The mask is checked at the destination cell only (Part 2: `classFactor(b)`, "the Horn slope at b"); a `natural_line` cliff or a `water` stream rasterised at 13 m is one cell wide; a (2,1) step crosses it, a (1,1) step passes between two diagonal mask cells. The "cliff line that blocks the cut" test as written would pass with the cut taken over the cliff. *Resolve:* rasterise impassable lines and polygon edges 8-connected (dilate by one cell across the step direction), allow a knight's move only when the two cells it crosses are passable, and make the synthetic cliff test a diagonal one-cell line so it can fail.

3. **Serious. The setting's second level changes the time model, not only the choice.** "Short cuts only" sets `P` = 600 and "class factors x 1.5"; Part 5 times a stretch by "the same factors as the cost, so the time the card shows and the choice the router made agree". So the same off-trail kilometre is shown 50 % slower under one setting than the other, and the fitted factors of Part 5 (the user's own ratio) are overridden by a preference. A setting that alters a displayed estimate is not what ADR 0044 means by a setting over behaviour. *Resolve:* the setting moves `P` (and, if a second knob is wanted, a minimum saving before a cut is taken), never the class factors; the time uses the model's factors (fitted when there are any) under both levels; the help text then reads true.

4. **Serious. "The pack's vector classes win where both say something" is the wrong rule for generic polygons.** The pack's `landcover` is OSM `natural=wood` and friends, generalised and merged below z13 (PROFILE line 76); an OSM wood polygon drawn at map scale covers clearings, meadows and talus that WorldCover resolves at 10 m. "Mapped by a person" does not make a 1:50,000 polygon finer than a 10 m pixel. Where the pack says `wood` (1.7) and WorldCover says grassland (1.3) or shrubland (2.5), the pack is the likelier error. *Resolve:* precedence by specificity: pack subclasses WorldCover cannot tell (`scree`, `shingle`, `bare_rock`, `glacier` against snow, `bog`/`marsh`/`swamp`) and pack `water`, `natural_line` and `landuse` built-up win; for tree, shrub, grass and bare, WorldCover wins where present; the first task reports the disagreement rate between the two on the Rainier box as a number, and the ADR carries it.

5. **Serious. `derived` is the wrong home for a source archive, a `PackRole` outside `files` is incoherent, and the manifest bump is avoidable.** `DerivedFile` is defined as "a file made from a pack's archives, never one of them", matched by `key` fingerprints to the archives it was derived from (`PackDerived.kt` lines 6 to 12); a WorldCover archive derives from nothing in the pack and has no key. `CatalogStatus.of` keys on `contentHash` over `files` (`PackCatalog.kt` lines 274 to 286): a pack that gains a land-cover archive after a phone installed it reads `INSTALLED` for ever and the archive is never fetched (ADR 0049's review, objection 5, the same hole; `PackManager.delete` and `PackNames.group` do not know a third file either). ADR 0049 recorded "the graph is never a `PackRole`" because `PackHash.combine` iterates `PackRole.entries` and every role enumerator (names, precedence, downloader, tile service) would learn it; the proposal creates `PackRole.LANDCOVER` and then keeps it out of `files`, which is the worst of both. On the manifest, `FileItem.parse` accepts any role string (`AreaManifest.kt` lines 29 to 39) and `RegionDownload` skips a `pack` item of a role it does not know (claim 8): a `kind: pack, role: landcover` item is parsed by every shipped build and ignored by older ones with **no version bump**, where the proposed new item kind throws in `ManifestItem.parse` and so forces version 4, refusing the whole manifest on every build to date (`checkVersion`, line 284) for any area that carries land cover. *Resolve:* decide one of two coherent shapes and say why: (i) the archive is a third `files` role (then it is part of the pack's identity and hash, older catalog parsers skip the entry, so it ships only in new packs or the catalog lists two entries); or (ii) an "optional files" list beside `files` with its own small match rule (bounds and zoom) and the `ensureRegion` fetch rule ADR 0049's review asked for, and `PackRole` stays two entries, the file named `<name>-landcover.pmtiles` by a separate parser. In either case the manifest item is `kind: pack` with `role: landcover`, and the version stays at 3. Name `PackNames`, `PackManager.delete`, the downloader's job type, the peer catalog and the import flow as moving parts.

6. **Serious. F1's off-trail marks live in a field designed to be dropped, and regeneration cannot recreate them.** `directions` is "never required for correctness": absent or stale, every build regenerates it from the graph (`Directions.of`), and `Directions.of` asks the graph for a junction at each point and says nothing elsewhere (lines 400, 459), so a bushwhack route whose directions are missing (a value over `MAX_CHARS`, line 309; a path written by a writer that does not write directions; a plan whose leg was spliced at a via point within `EDGE_M`) is drawn solid with ordinary directions **on the planning build too**, with no notice. The geometry of which points are off trail is not recoverable from the path alone. *Resolve:* keep F1, and give the new build a regeneration rule that does not depend on the field: `Directions.of` for a `bushwhack` path classifies each point by distance to the nearest graph edge (`Router.nearest`, `DEFAULT_SNAP_M` scaled down to a few metres) and emits `LEAVE_TRAIL` and `REJOIN_TRAIL` at the transitions, so a lost field is rebuilt the same way directions are today; test it by decoding a bushwhack fixture with its `directions` removed. State in the ADR that older builds lose the marks (already accepted) and that the stretch figures on the card come from the regenerated list when the saved one is absent.

7. **Serious. The 40 degree cut on a surface model turns forest edges into walls.** The Copernicus DEM is a DSM with canopy (`sources.json`, claim 18); every pack outside 3DEP (Mont Blanc, Glacier/Waterton's Canadian side, all of Europe) prices a 25 m tree line at a clearing's edge as a 13 m step of 20 m or more: over 40 degrees, impassable in both the step slope and the Horn slope. The model would then forbid leaving a trail into any meadow from the forest, which is the opposite of the terrain's truth, and the refusal would read "No way across: water, cliffs or glacier". *Resolve:* the first task measures the impassable fraction of the window and the length of impassable boundary on a Copernicus-only area from `areas.json` at the same zoom; the ADR decides a DSM rule before the mask is trusted outside 3DEP (candidates: the cut only where the pack's `natural_line` or `rock`/`bare_rock` agrees under tree cover; a longer slope baseline (3 cells) under WorldCover class 10; a per-source `demKind` the registry already implies). The report names the DEM source under the window in `grid.dem`.

8. **Serious. A heap refusal is routed to the wrong sentence and has no named lift.** Part 1 C: "past it again refuses with 'Too far for an off-trail route (36 km): add a stop in between'" covers the cell cap; "when pass 2 is refused for size, pass 1's trail route is returned with a notice" conflates the cap (a constant, lift: a stop) with `HeapGuard` refusing a 10 km window because the app is heavy now (lift: not a stop). A user told to add a stop to a 10 km leg adds one and is refused again. Release builds have no lift for a heap refusal today (ADR 0049's review, Part 4); the debug lift `force=1` exists for `POST /route/build` only. *Resolve:* two sentences and two codes: the cap's as written; the heap's "Not enough memory for an off-trail route right now (needs 28 MB, 12 MB free): try again in a moment" with the admission in `GET /route/status` and `POST /route?mode=bushwhack&force=1` in debug builds, documented in `android-debug-routes.md`; a pass 1 route returned in either case is labelled "Planned along trails" with the reason code.

9. **Serious. Hidden coupling: the plan's offline download is narrower than the window.** ADR 0040: "the plan's offline download is the current path's corridor plus a box around each stop"; T130's corridor is 1 km. A bushwhack leg replanned offline from the fix (ADR 0040's automatic replan) needs the DEM at z13, the vector tiles at z14 and the land-cover tiles over a window up to 3 km each side; the corridor has none past 1 km, `DemPyramid.window` returns NaN there, and the replan either refuses ("No terrain data here") or silently searches a window clipped to the corridor and misses the cut it found online. This decision therefore decides the corridor width per leg mode. *Resolve:* say so in the ADR: a plan with a bushwhack leg downloads the leg's window (the pass 1 line and the straight line buffered by `W`) at the three zooms, the size shown first (ADR 0023); until that lands, a bushwhack replan offline falls to pass 1 with the sentence naming why; the corridor manifest (ADR 0030) gains the leg's `W` as a field or the leg's own corridor item.

10. **Serious. Glaciers and permanent snow impassable by default blocks the mode where it is most wanted, and WorldCover cannot tell the two apart.** Class 70 is "snow and ice" at 10 m from 2021 imagery: permanent snowfields, late-lying snow and glaciers alike; the pack's `landcover` class `ice` is OSM `natural=glacier`, which mappers also draw over snowfields. On Rainier the high ground above Panorama Point is class 70 for kilometres; the model's answer to any high off-trail leg there is "No way across: water, cliffs or glacier block every off-trail line here", and the lift (a third setting level) is left open. *Resolve:* propose to the owner, with the reasons, that pack `glacier` polygons are impassable by default (crevasses are the safety case) and WorldCover class 70 without a pack glacier is passable at a high factor (say 2.0) with the card line "crosses permanent snow" and the exposure figures, and that the third level ("... including glaciers") is built in the first phone task rather than deferred, so the default has its lift from day one; measure the class 70 share of the Rainier box in the first task and put the number in the ADR.

11. **Minor. `Instruction` has no point index; drawing from directions is new on both renderers.** Claim 7. *Resolve:* correct the text ("drawn from the instructions' `alongM` projected onto the line"); list `TripOverlay` (a second dashed layer with its own style, filtered on an `offtrail` feature property the phone computes) and `trips.js` as moving parts; the T108 rule that saved directions are "quantised as the field keeps it" means the projection tolerates decimetres.

12. **Minor. The window is sized from `D` alone and misses the horseshoe cut.** Part 2 of this review. *Resolve:* `W` from `max(D, pass1 / 2)`; snap pass 1 at `FAR_SEARCH_M` when an end is off network before falling to the straight line; the synthetic valley test gets a horseshoe variant whose cut lies outside `0.3 x D` of both lines.

13. **Minor. The 40 degree cut rests on the survey's memory and the readout already has two other thresholds.** Claim 15: "the SAC T4 to T5 boundary" is an `[M]` row; the exposure readout says "over 30" and "over 35"; the cost ramps from 30 and cuts at 40. *Resolve:* mark the number as the project's own choice for the owner (it is on the owner's list anyway), cite nothing for it, and align the card's lines with the cost's two numbers or explain the three.

14. **Minor. The paletted class tile is not readable by the shared PNG decoder.** Claim 17. *Resolve:* either extend `PngDecoder` to colour types 0 and 3 at bit depth 8 (pure Kotlin, a few lines, with a test), or write the tiles as 8-bit grey; say which, and that the fallback decoder is not the plan (the devtool and the server would then read them through ImageIO and the phone through BitmapFactory, two code paths for one byte).

15. **Minor. NaN cells and the DEM's edge.** `DemPyramid.window` leaves NaN where nothing covers; the proposal says the window is clipped to coverage and "gives up where the DEM does". *Resolve:* state the rule (a NaN cell is impassable; a window with over half its cells NaN is refused with the no-DEM sentence); the user sentence for a window clipped at a pack's edge names the lift ("download a larger area" or the area's name).

16. **Minor. The cost and the time disagree by `steepFactor`.** Part 2 multiplies length by `steepFactor` (1.0 to 4.0) in the cost; Part 5 times a stretch by "the class factors" only, yet claims the two agree. *Resolve:* say which: either `steepFactor` is a time factor too (then the card's time on a 35 degree slope triples, and the fitted off-trail factor should absorb it) or it is a preference against steep ground that the time does not show (then say "the router avoids ground over 30 degrees beyond what it costs in time").

17. **Minor. Coarsening to z12 is silent, and the cap has no test lift.** Past 1.5 M cells pass 2 "coarsens once to z12" with no word to the user; the JSON gets `grid.zoom` but the card does not. The cap is a constant with no debug route to lower it, so the refusal path is tested only with a 36 km leg. *Resolve:* the card's stretch line says "(coarse terrain grid)" when z12 was used; `POST /route/grid?maxCells=` (debug, like `POST /route/guard?maxBytes=`) and `devtool route --grid-cells` for the tests.

18. **Minor. The z13 archive's resampling rule, the registry `kind`, and the tile list.** A 10 m raster at 13 to 19 m cells needs a stated rule (the mode of the pixels under the cell, not nearest, so a thin class does not flicker); `sources.json`'s header comment lists the allowed `kind` values and must gain `landcover`; the bucket lists anonymously (claim 10), so the registry needs the URL pattern and the listing endpoint, not 2,651 names. *Resolve:* three lines in L1.

19. **Minor. The bushwhack model must alias the hike fit, and the factors' home needs a line.** Claim 16: `CostModel(TravelMode.BUSHWHACK)` gets no `PaceFit` unless `FITTED` or the constructor says so; CROW shows the trap (Naismith with a fit present). The factors can ride as appended hike fields or a `bushwhack:` entry; either is tolerated by older decoders. *Resolve:* say `CostModel(BUSHWHACK)` uses the hike fit; pick the field placement; one test that an older-format value still decodes.

20. **Minor. The mode is defensible; the proposal should state what it costs and does not buy.** A chip plus a two-level setting is the survey's three-way setting split over two surfaces; the T108 protection covers routes and not legs inside plans (ADR 0040, stated); a fifth chip at font scale 2.0 is the layout T212 is now fixing; every `when (mode)` grows an arm. *Resolve:* keep the mode, add these to "What would change", and ask the owner the one question that matters: whether a bushwhack route edited on a phone that does not know the mode should be refused (the mode) or re-routed along trails (the setting).

21. **Minor. No dated, failable predictions.** "What would change this recommendation" is good and measurable (10 s, 40 MB), but nothing is written as a prediction with a check date as ADRs 0039, 0044 and 0049 carry. *Resolve:* three for the ADR: the 10 km row's peak bytes and seconds on the API 35 emulator against the fitted weight (check when task one merges); the Rainier box's pack-versus-WorldCover disagreement rate and class 70 share (task one); within two waves of the phone task, one real off-trail track of the owner's matched into pieces with a factor that differs from 1.67 (task three).

22. **Minor. Dependency checklist item 7 under-answers the parser change.** "nothing to parse beyond the TIFF the reader already guards" is true of the data and false of the reader: an 8-bit path with predictor 2 on bytes, a palette tag the reader ignores, `TileByteCounts` trusted for the buffer size. *Resolve:* answer 7 for the new path (bounds on byte counts, a truncated tile, a wrong `BitsPerSample`) and add the `GeoTiffTest` cases; note the correction in the proposal's favour that deflate is already in commonMain (`RemoteTiff.kt` line 92).

## Part 5. Hidden coupling

- The corridor download's width becomes per leg mode (objection 9); ADR 0030's corridor manifest and ADR 0040's plan download inherit `W`.
- The pack format: a third file is either a role in `files` (identity and hash move) or an optional list with a match rule (objection 5); whichever is chosen is the pattern the next optional archive (a search index, contours) will copy, as ADR 0049's review warned about `derived`.
- `TravelMode` as the key of `PaceFits.active` and of the setting value means a mode that borrows another's fit needs an explicit alias (objection 19); a later "snowshoe" or "ski" mode meets the same question.
- `directions` as the carrier of safety marks makes the field load-bearing for a feature for the first time (objection 6); the regeneration rule proposed there is what keeps ADR 0040's "never required" true.
- The land-cover archive at z13 only pins the grid's zoom to the archive's: a z14 grid (6.5 m, the cap at a quarter of the area) would upsample classes; the ADR should say the grid zoom is the archive's zoom or coarser.
- A new `Maneuver` pair is the first since T102 that older builds refuse; from now on every `directions` value a new phone writes for a bushwhack route is unreadable on the previous release, which makes the "one release behind" case ADR 0040 cares about a real test fixture (the proposal names the `sync-sim` round trip; keep it).

## Part 6. Pre-mortem

Ten years on, the off-trail mode is the feature testers mention first and the one the project would unship if it could: every phone still runs a 13 m grid whose impassable mask was drawn once from a 2021 land-cover classification and a surface model with trees in it, so the router walks confidently through young regrowth it calls grassland and refuses to leave the trail at every forest edge in the Alps; the "pack wins" rule meant that where a volunteer had drawn a wood polygon over a whole valley in 2014, the meadows inside it were priced as forest for a decade; the dashed stretches vanished whenever a trip's directions were rebuilt, so users learned that a solid line might be off trail; and the memory guard admitted every window because the weight it was asked for was a sixth of what the search allocated, until a 20 km leg on a small phone reproduced the 2026 crash with `admitted: true` in the log. The line that should have caught it is in Part 1 C: "holds per cell: elevation (Float, 4 B), class (1 B), g (4 B), parent (4 B) and two flag bits: about 14 B, call it 16 B with the open heap", written beside a search that "stays optimal" by the bidirectional argument whose per-node state is 42 B three screens away in the file the proposal cites; and, for the mask, "The pack's vector classes win where both say something (they are mapped by a person; WorldCover is a classifier)", a sentence that mistakes provenance for resolution.

## Recommendation

C with F1 and L3 then L1 is the right shape, and the proposal is right that the format needs no version step, that the raster is CC BY 4.0 and anonymous (now confirmed against the bytes), and that the mode rather than a setting is defensible for routes. I would not promote it as written. Three corrections change what gets built: pick one search and fit its weight before the cap is a number (objection 1); make the mask barrier-safe against knight's moves and diagonals (objection 2) and decide a surface-model rule before the 40 degree cut leaves 3DEP coverage (objection 7); and put the archive in a coherent place in the pack format with a `pack` manifest item and no version bump (objection 5). Three more change what users see: the setting moves `P` and not the time model (objection 3), the off-trail marks are regenerable from the geometry and the graph (objection 6), and the glacier default is proposed to the owner with the snowfield case and its lift built in the same task (objection 10). The window formula, the heap sentence and the corridor coupling (objections 8, 9, 12) are lines in the ADR and the first brief. Residual risks after all of that: the class factors are guesses until tracks replace them, and the proposal says so plainly; the first phone task will find the card crowded at font scale 2.0 with a fifth chip and two stretch lines; and the pass 1 plus pass 2 cost on a 30 km leg is unmeasured and may push the common long case to z12 always, which is acceptable only if the card says so. Confidence: high that C with these corrections is the right decision; moderate on the glacier default (the owner's, argued here on one mountain); low on the 16 B figure ever being reached, which is why objection 1 comes first.
