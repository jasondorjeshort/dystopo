### T309. A Map | Horizon button on the map, and the closest peaks first on the rim
**Goal:** two halves, peaks first as their own commit, then the button. (1) **Peaks** (`shared/.../horizon/PeakRim.kt`): candidates are every named peak within the 30 km range that is visible (`PeakView.visible`) or only just out of sight, hidden by no more than a height: `clearanceDeg >= -atan(HIDDEN_M / distanceM)` with `HIDDEN_M` = 60 m (a constant: DEM cells and shaved tops are tens of metres off; an angle would hide 17 m at 500 m and 870 m at 25 km); `clearanceDeg` +90 (no blocker marched) counts as visible. The rim's names: first the highest skyline peak of the whole view (from Paradise, Mount Rainier; the owner's "closest first" sets the order, it does not drop the summit that fills the sky), then the **closest** candidate of each 30° sector, then the remaining room filled closest first wherever a name collides with no other name, no ring label, no hour label and not the sun's arc (this also settles T308's landscape and winter collisions). A candidate out of sight is a hollow triangle at its own apparent angle; its row and pick say "just out of sight". The plot's names, the pick, the list (sorted by distance, closest first), `GET /horizon`'s `peaks`, the help text `horizon.peaks` and `HorizonText`'s count and none lines all move from skyline peaks to the candidates: "N peaks in view or just out of sight within 30 km". (2) **The button**: a two-segment "Map | Horizon" on the map screen, a new element of the layout authority placed by `arrangeDrawn` (the live placer) in the left lower column above the scale bar's line, yielding after the scale bar and before the readout's core entries, hidden with a named reason where that rung says so (landscape with a route card or the long-press panel up, font scale 2.0 in landscape), never overlapping. "Horizon" opens the Horizon view (T308). On the Horizon view the same control is fixed at the bottom of the content pane (the pane below the square in portrait, the right pane in landscape), "Horizon" selected, the scroll area padded so its last row clears it; "Map" closes the view, as Close and Back do. The Settings row stays. No compass shortcut: MapLibre's compass shows only when the map is turned and its tap resets north.
Changed 2026-11-20, after launch, on the owner's word: "I think we instead want some sort of algorithm to prioritize peaks (and more later) based on distance and height above horizon. Then put the highest ones up based on some sort of density function. Should research this in some way, but...I dunno. It's a display question." So the peak half becomes a generic priority score in `shared/.../horizon/` (a `HorizonLabel` candidate of any kind; the score from distance and height above or below the horizon, the dominant summit boosted, weights as named constants in one place) and a placement pass, highest score first, that accepts a label only where the local label density allows (collision and spacing against placed labels, ring and hour labels, the sun's arc), replacing the fixed 30° sectors; the candidate filter (visible, or out of sight by no more than 60 m), the hollow triangle and "just out of sight" stay. The weights come from a read-only research agent (technical variant, ADR 0044), relayed to the implementer; the tests check the mechanics, not provisional weights. The button half goes first.
