it worked from search results, and it marks which figures are sourced and which are its own arithmetic: the KDoc of each named constant says which, with the range): lateral limit 2.0 m/s2 up to 60 km/h falling linearly to 1.5 at 100 km/h (design side-friction values fall with speed; range 1.5 to 2.5, so the idea's 2.5 is the top); the radius from the line resampled to about 10 m, the circumradius over points 20 to 30 m either side, the median of three neighbouring radii, under 3 degrees per 20 m read as straight, capped at 1,000 m (the survey's own method: closely spaced nodes otherwise give radii far too small); forward limit 1.5 m/s2 at low speed falling linearly to 0.8 at 100 km/h (the fall is sourced, the end value is not; range 1.0 to 2.0); braking a flat 2.0 m/s2 (range 1.5 to 3.0); a priced turn's speed by its angle, interpolated: none under 20 degrees, 35 km/h at 45, 20 km/h at 90 on the kerb side and 15 across traffic, 12 at 135, 8 for a reversal (the survey's own table, between field figures of 14.5 to 34 km/h for a right-angle turn), the through exit and a junction of two roads unpriced as in `TurnCost`. **Time:** the integral, plus for a turn across traffic only the wait, taken as the table's seconds across traffic less its kerb-side seconds at that angle (the slowing and the regain are already in the profile, so the table's full seconds would count them twice; the coordinator's reading, not sourced: the finding gives the time with and without it). **Fuel:** idle power over the whole profile's time plus, per step, the traction work where it is positive: resistance, the kinetic term and the grade together, floored at zero (a descent lowers what the engine gives down to nothing and never below; braking and coasting earn nothing back; this replaces the table model's capped descent credit inside the profile, and the grade is each edge row's rise spread over its length, so T286's portal rule holds); no per-turn regain charge (the profile holds it). **The class unsteadiness factor** of `FuelModel.edgeJ` stands in for stops the model cannot see: the profile's own figure sets it to 1 (it models the unsteadiness it can see, and stops and signals are out of pass one), and the finding gives the figure with the factor kept beside it, so the gap is on the page. **The limits are a parameter object** with these first values as its defaults: the tests of textbook cases run it with flat limits, and devtool takes the low and the high end of each range by switches (`route --profile-limits low|first|high`, no setting). None of these is a user setting (physical constants; the survey found no app offering a driving style; ADR 0044). **What a believable saving is:** the survey's published range for least fuel against the fastest route is about 3 to 10 percent for a petrol car (one study 8.2 percent on the trips where the routes differed), so the finding treats 33 % and 47 % as a model error unless it shows a physical cause. Devtool `route --profile` prints `profile` on a drive (time, fuel, the points where the profile is held down by a bend, a turn or the edge's speed, counted, and, when the plan is not least time, the percentage against the least-time route's profile, for which devtool plans that route too as T290's `--against` does; the percentage may be negative and is printed so) beside today's figures, which do not change. Out of scope: the phone, the search, stops and signals, the card. The idea file stays (its later passes are re-planned by the coordinator from the finding).
