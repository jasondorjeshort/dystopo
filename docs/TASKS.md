# Task board

Work is split into tasks that don't depend on each other, so several agents can run at once. One agent per task, one git worktree per agent; see `CLAUDE.md` › Working in parallel. Closed waves are in `docs/tasks/`, one record each; this board holds the open wave, Owner actions pending, Later and the follow-up lists.

Every task is done only when:
1. `./gradlew check` passes (existing tests plus new ones).
2. An agent can exercise the feature from the command line and see the result as JSON or PNG.
3. The implementer's report (the merge commit's body, ADR 0043) says what was measured (timings, counts, image observations), using only public areas from `areas.json`.

"Owns" lists the files a task may change freely. Touching anything else should be a few lines at most and called out in the report. Two tasks that both need `MapRenderer.kt` are told below which part is theirs.

**Interacts with:** (optional) names another task of the wave and the mechanism the two share although their Owns lines are disjoint (a hook, a cache, a format, an index, a join); the verifier of whichever merges second runs one scenario across both (ADR 0048).

### T178. The storm warning from the barometer's trend (after T177)
**Goal:** `docs/ideas/2026-10-30-storm-warning-from-the-barometer-trend.md` (the owner: "fascinating, but remember we're gaining thousands of feet, so use QNH") with the technical survey (`out/wave23-research/storm-warning.md`) and the critic's corrections (`critic-review.md` § 1 and 12), which together replace the idea Plan's reference-height rule: a fall of 4 hPa in 3 h is the near-universal watch default, at the bottom of the Met Office "falling quickly" band; no watch claims to detect weather while climbing, Suunto counts only still periods; the sensor is not the limit (±0.03 hPa relative, a stable bias); a height error moves the reduced figure by 0.12 hPa per m; a temperature error of 10 K over a 1000 m net climb is about 4 hPa of false trend whatever the datum, so the temperature is the trap and the reduction can only be as good as the temperature it uses. New `shared/.../readout/PressureTrend.kt` (pure, unit-tested): one median per minute of pressure paired with the median height of that minute's fixes, the height from the DEM at the fix on foot (`LiveReadout`'s `ElevationSampler`; 3DEP bare earth in CONUS, Copernicus a surface model elsewhere, so ±15 m under forest there, the source per region named in the report), GNSS height only as a fallback that suppresses the alert unless the walker was still, never the readout's fused elevation or its offset (circular: the offset absorbs the weather); each minute reduced to the window's median height by the hypsometric equation with one fixed temperature per window: the station's last temperature lapsed at 6.5 K/km when a station reading exists (the METAR temperature group, parsed in `Altimeter.kt`), else the standard atmosphere with σ_T = 10 K, never a live temperature; the tendency as the difference of robust means of the first and last half-hour (end to end, as the WMO tendency is), plus a 1 h check at half the threshold, the severer shown (a weighted 3 h line hides a fast fall in the last hour); an interval per window: the random term plus the systematic terms added, not averaged (the height bias 0.12 hPa/m × the height's bias bound, the temperature term 0.0043 × |net climb in km| × σ_T/10 K × p); when both end half-hours are still (under 5 m of vertical movement in 12 min) the tendency from raw pressure with no height at all, else the reduced series; the alert when the fall passes the threshold and the interval's lower bound passes 75% of it; the arrow by the Met Office bands (steady, falling slowly, falling, falling fast, falling very fast at 0.1, 1.6, 3.6 and 6 hPa); the sea-level equivalent (× p0/p) shown so it matches a forecast; the first 10 s after a listener start dropped, minute medians jumping over 1.5 hPa against their neighbours dropped, minutes faster than 7 m/s excluded; Sampling: `Barometer.want` takes reasons (`readout`, `trend`); while a track records (T159's service) the trend keeps 1 Hz with 4 s batching or 1 Hz for 30 s of each minute, the wake-up sensor preferred, confirmed with the screen off on the device; not otherwise.
